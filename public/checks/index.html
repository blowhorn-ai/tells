Blowhorn Tells

What each check means

Each check has a page here: what it reads, how Pass, Warning, Fail and Not available are decided, and the points a finding subtracts. The same list is the machine-readable catalog schemas/check-catalog.json, catalog version 1.0.3. Changelog.

How a result is decided

Every check ends in one of four states. Pass: the readings were collected and nothing in them matched a known tell. Warning: a medium or low severity finding matched. Fail: a high severity finding matched, the kind a real gate would act on. Not available: the readings never arrived (the browser does not expose them, this site's plan does not include them, or a drill was not taken). Not available is a statement about coverage, never a failure.

Findings also carry points. The verdict starts at 100 and each finding subtracts its weight, so one page's Fail on a heavyweight check can cost more than three Warnings elsewhere. Each check page lists the findings that move its row.

Automation flags

  • Managed bot score: A score of 50 or better.
  • Browser data: The self-description is ordinary and internally consistent.
  • Remote-control tells: No machinery found and no patch seams showing.

Behavior

  • Behavioural classification: A score of 0.5 or better. A short, still visit scores lower than an active one; that is the measure working, not an accusation.
  • Interaction: The task was completed and the evidence matched a person.

Fingerprint consistency

  • Request profile: Request and browser describe the same browser on the same platform.
  • Proxy indicators: Only Cloudflare's normal forwarding headers, or none at all.
  • Datacenter pattern: The owner reads as a consumer or business ISP.
  • VPN / proxy / Tor intelligence: No flags raised.
  • TLS fingerprint: Tells displays the fingerprint but does not score it: on its own it identifies a class of software, not a culprit.
  • Browser fingerprint: Both probes rendered and hashed normally.
  • Fingerprint protection: Every surface read steadily, or some were randomized or blocked. Randomized and blocked surfaces are named in the result but cost nothing, because privacy browsers and anti-detection browsers both randomize.
  • Fingerprint surfaces: The surfaces are reported, not scored on their own. Their weight is in combination: each is a small fact, and together they single out one browser in a crowd.
  • Performance memory pings: Every sample is physically plausible.
  • Worker consistency: Every context that answered agrees.
  • WebRTC network clues: WebRTC's public addresses agree with the request, or it exposed only local addresses.
  • Latency: The two clocks roughly agree.

Which checks does a platform care about?

Not every site weighs these equally. The platform guide ranks the platforms Blowhorn works with by how hard their defences lean on each family of tells, and explains why an IP finding can end the conversation on one platform while behaviour decides it on another.
